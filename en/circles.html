Circles of An

The rhythm of a day and a week, and how to practise together in a small group. No leader needed, no contribution asked.

The rhythm of a day

  1. Morning

    Read the morning Words of An. A few minutes of stillness before you pick up your phone.

  2. During the day

    Whatever you do, be wholly in it. When tense: count 1 to 6, ask “I or Want?”

  3. Afternoon

    One act of Hiếu or kindness: a call, a thank-you.

  4. Evening

    Write three enoughs. Read the evening Words of An.

Words of An

Two short passages to open and close a day. Read them silently or aloud.

Morning

Today I begin from An.

I set down the word “I”: no need to be more, no need to be right.

I set down the word “Want”: no chasing what does not suit me.

What remains is happiness, right now.

Evening

Today I had enough.

I am grateful to those who gave me this day.

What I did wrong, I will mend tomorrow.

I return to An.

Start a Circle of An

A Circle of An is 5 to 12 people who meet for an hour each week, in person or online. Anyone can start one: family, friends, colleagues.

Stillness5′Reading15′Sharing25′Intention10′Words of An5′
  1. 5 min

    Stillness

    Sit quietly together, counting silently from 1 to 6.

  2. 15 min

    Reading

    One person reads a teaching aloud. In order, from lesson 1.

  3. 25 min

    Sharing

    Each person: one moment “I” or “Want” spoke up this week, and three enoughs.

  4. 10 min

    Intention

    Each person chooses one act of Hiếu or kindness for the coming week.

  5. 5 min

    Words of An

    Read the evening Words of An together.

Five agreements

  1. Listen; do not advise, do not judge.
  2. What is said in the circle stays in the circle.
  3. Anyone may stay silent. Anyone may leave.
  4. No money collected, nothing sold.
  5. Nobody is the teacher. The timekeeper rotates weekly.

The milestones of a life

One simple form for every occasion: a minute of stillness, a word of gratitude, a promise.

Birth
The parents thank the grandparents, and promise to live so that the child can be at ease.
Coming of age
The young person writes their three principles for living and reads them to the family.
Becoming a couple
Each tells the other what they will let go: their own share of “I” and of “Want”.
Farewell
Each person tells one thing the one who has died left in them.

Nine commitments

Every activity carried out in the name of the Peaceful Way keeps these nine.

  1. 1Voluntary
  2. 2No monopoly on truth
  3. 3No personality cult
  4. 4Financial transparency
  5. 5No selling of fear
  6. 6No substitute for medicine
  7. 7Protection of the vulnerable
  8. 8Respect for the law
  9. 9Open to criticism
