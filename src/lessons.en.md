# I want happiness

> Drop “I”, drop “Want”, and only Happiness remains.

Everything in the Peaceful Way begins with a sentence everyone has said: *I want happiness.*

Look at it closely. It has three parts. The first is **I**. The middle is **Want**. The last is **Happiness**. Happiness is already in the sentence, but it stands behind the other two words, and those two words are what hide it.

- Dropping **I** is dropping the ego, the self.
- Dropping **Want** is dropping greed, and wishes for what does not suit you.
- Only **Happiness** remains, and it is happiness in the present, right now.

So the Peaceful Way does not teach how to *go and find* happiness. Happiness is not at the end of a long road. It is what is left when two burdens are set down.

To set them down, we need to understand the Circle of Hiếu Đạo, with An, peace, at its centre. The lessons that follow go through it part by part.

**Practice.** Write “I want happiness” on paper. Cross out “I”. Cross out “want”. Look at the word that is left for one minute.

**Ask yourself.** Right now, adding nothing and proving nothing, what am I lacking?

# I: the ego

> When the ego rules, people place their own needs and wants above everything, and that causes conflict and discord.

The ego is the self, self-esteem, the way we see ourselves in relation to the world. Everyone has one, and a modest ego is normal.

Suffering begins when the ego **inflates**. Then it produces three things: selfishness, arrogance, and a lack of empathy. A person with a large ego always has to be right, to win, to be seen. They are tired, and so are the people around them.

The clearest signs: you find it hard to apologise, you are easily hurt by criticism, and you keep comparing yourself with others.

Dropping “I” is not erasing yourself. You are still you. What is set down is only the inflated part. When it shrinks, you can hear other people, and for the first time you see what you need to let go of. That is the door to a meaningful life.

**Practice.** Today, in one conversation, let the other person finish before you answer. Just once.

**Ask yourself.** When did I last need to win? What did I gain afterwards?

# Want: greed

> Greed means a person never feels satisfied with what they have and always wants more, which leads to unrest and suffering.

Greed is excessive desire. It is not only greed for money and property. It is also the craving for power, fame and other people's attention.

Greed works in a very simple way: it keeps moving the goal. Reach one thing and it points to the next. So the greedy person never arrives, and their joy always lives in tomorrow.

Dropping “Want” does not mean having no wishes. What is let go is greed, and **the wishes that do not suit you**. Once those are gone, you can see what you truly want, and it is usually simpler than you expected. That is the door to true happiness.

**Practice.** Write down five things you want. For each, ask: does this suit me? Keep the ones that do. Cross out the ones that do not.

**Ask yourself.** The thing I am chasing: once I have it, will I stop?

# Only happiness: contentment

> The core of the Peaceful Way is contentment: becoming aware of, and satisfied with, what one has, and from that creating peace and harmony in the soul and in life.

When “I” and “Want” step back, what remains has a very ordinary name: **enough**.

Contentment (Biết Đủ, “knowing enough”) means gratitude and satisfaction with what you already have. It is not resignation, and it is not giving up effort. A contented person still works hard. The difference is that they do not need the result before they are allowed to feel at peace.

True happiness, in the Peaceful Way, comes from simple, practical things: a meal, a night's sleep, someone to call. Most of us already have these. Contentment is the act of seeing them.

That is why happiness in the Peaceful Way is always in the present tense. Not “one day I will be happy”, but “right now”.

**Practice.** Tonight, write three things you had enough of today.

**Ask yourself.** For me, how much is enough?

# An is the root

> “An” is the root and the centre. Everything good and of real value in life begins from a peaceful, still inner state.

An is peace within. The Peaceful Way places this word at the centre of the Circle of Hiếu Đạo for one reason: every other good thing grows from here.

Try thinking of it the other way round. A devoted child with no peace inside finds devotion a burden. A rich person with no peace inside still feels short. Without An, every other value is unsteady.

When we are at peace, we understand ourselves. Only when we truly touch An do we enter the journey of discovering ourselves and living a meaningful life.

The Circle of Hiếu Đạo is an endless cycle: An gives rise to Hiếu, then Đạo, Đức, Phúc, Tài, and returns to An. It always begins and ends at An.

**Practice.** Three times today, stop for ten seconds and ask only: is my mind at rest?

**Ask yourself.** What unsettles my mind most? Is it “I” or “Want”?

# Hiếu: filial devotion

> Hiếu is a serene inner state that lets one's parents feel at ease, and through that we too find real peace in our soul.

Hiếu is respecting and loving one's parents, elders, and those who have done one good. It is the first word of the circle, where peace steps out of the inner life and into the closest relationship a person has.

The Peaceful Way understands Hiếu in its own way. It is not something forced by duty. Nor is it measured by the money or status we bring home. Its measure is: **are my parents at ease about me?**

When a child lives peacefully, kindly and steadily, the parents are at ease. And when the parents are at ease, the child's heart is lighter. Hiếu is therefore a two-way road of peace.

Hiếu never obliges anyone to endure harm. For someone whose family circumstances are difficult, it can still be practised with those who have done them good.

**Practice.** Call a parent, or someone who once helped you. Ask how they are, then listen.

**Ask yourself.** What about me is keeping my parents from being at ease?

# Đạo: the Way

> A person who truly understands and lives by the Peaceful Way is not shaken by outside forces. They stay steady and serene however many hardships life brings.

Đạo is pursuing the path of ethics and ideals in life. Once Hiếu is practised, we come to Đạo: a way of living led by inner peace itself.

Everyone meets difficulty. The difference between a person with a Way and a person without one is not who meets fewer storms. It is who still has a direction when the storm comes.

A person without a Way is pushed around by circumstances: glad when praised, low when blamed, proud in gain, crushed in loss. A person with a Way knows where they are going, so those things cannot move them.

**Practice.** Write down three principles for living that you would not trade for any benefit.

**Ask yourself.** Was my most recent decision one I chose, or one that circumstances pushed me into?

# Đức: virtue

> Đức is not only outward good qualities; it is a state of soul that is balanced, kind and sincere with everyone around.

Đức is developing and maintaining noble moral qualities. If Đạo is the direction, Đức is the person you become after walking that road long enough.

Đức is formed from experience and deep self-awareness. So it cannot be memorised, and it cannot be performed. A person can appear kind, but real virtue comes from deep inner peace: they are kind when nobody is watching.

Three marks of Đức in the Peaceful Way: balance, kindness and sincerity.

**Practice.** Choose one quality for this week, such as keeping your word. Review it once each evening.

**Ask yourself.** Am I the same person when I am watched and when I am not?

# Phúc: blessing

> Phúc is not only wealth and material comfort; above all it is serenity, fulfilment, harmony in every relationship, and deep meaning in life.

Phúc is creating and experiencing blessings and happiness from ethical action. From Đức, life fills with Phúc.

Many people ask for blessing as if it were a gift from outside. The Peaceful Way sees it differently: blessing is something we **create**. A person who lives with virtue is trusted and loved, their relationships last, their heart is light. That is Phúc, and it arrives as a natural result.

It has three sides: serenity within, harmony with others, and a sense that one's life means something. A person can be very rich and lack all three.

**Practice.** Do one kind thing today without letting anyone know.

**Ask yourself.** Which of my relationships needs mending?

# Tài: abundance

> Tài here is not merely material; it is richness and variety of mind and feeling, and the ability to share with the community.

Tài is attaining wealth through contentment, not only in money but in every aspect of life. When Phúc is complete, Tài comes naturally.

This order is the most thought-provoking thing about the circle. Most of us put wealth first: earn the money, then think about ethics, family and peace. The Peaceful Way puts it last. Wealth comes afterwards, and it is measured by **enough**, not by **much**.

Tài is also wider than money. Understanding is wealth. A rich emotional life is wealth. And the ability to share with the community is its highest form, because only someone who feels they have enough can share.

Contented abundance returns us to An, and the circle begins again.

**Practice.** Share one thing you have enough of: time, knowledge, or money.

**Ask yourself.** In what way am I rich without ever having noticed?

# Know yourself: one destination

> Three purposes but one destination: reach self-understanding, and the other two are reached as well.

The Peaceful Way names three purposes: knowing yourself, true happiness, and a meaningful life. But they are not three peaks to climb one after another. There is only **one destination**: knowing yourself.

Knowing yourself means being aware of your own needs, wants and limits. When we are at peace, that understanding comes by itself. And from it the other two open:

- We know what we truly **want**, and so recognise **true happiness**.
- We know what we need to **let go** of, the ego, and so find **the meaning of life**.

True happiness is joy and satisfaction in simple, practical things. The meaning of life is found through action and relationships with others. Both are fruit. Self-understanding is the root.

**Practice.** Use the “I and Want mirror” on the Know yourself page.

**Ask yourself.** What do I really need? What do I want? Where are my limits?

# Stillness and Peaceful Meditation

> Meditation returns us to stillness, the original state of the soul, and from there re-establishes the balance of body and soul.

Peaceful Meditation (Thiền An) is not the centre of the Peaceful Way. It is a **supporting method** for the journey towards peace within.

Its value lies in stillness. The human body always has a way of restoring its own balance. But when we are too active, talking, thinking, moving, energy goes entirely to those things. Only when we stop does that energy turn to healing within.

The practice is very simple, and can be done **at any time, in any posture**:

- When doing nothing: count silently and slowly from 1 to 6, over and over, until the mind is empty.
- When working: give the task your whole attention, without thinking about the result.
- When the mind wanders: bring it back.
- After that there is nothing to do; just listen to your own body and mind.

**Practice.** Five minutes with the meditation timer.

**Ask yourself.** When did I last truly do nothing?
