## Abstract

Đạo An (the Peaceful Way) is a system of thought and a philosophy of living by Mr.33, within the Kabala ecosystem in Vietnam. Its main purpose: to help people recognise their own true happiness and live a meaningful life.

The whole system rests on four statements:

1. **Diagnosis.** All suffering in life grows from two roots: *Greed* and *Ego*.
2. **Core.** The remedy is *Contentment* (Biết Đủ, "knowing enough"): seeing and being at ease with what you already have.
3. **Destination.** Three purposes but one destination: *Know yourself*. Reach it, and *True happiness* and *A meaningful life* are reached too. In the sentence "I want happiness", drop *I* and drop *Want*, and only *Happiness* remains, right now.
4. **Structure of a life.** The *Circle of Hiếu Đạo*: from Peace comes gratitude to one's roots (Hiếu), then the Way (Đạo), Virtue (Đức), Blessing (Phúc), Abundance (Tài), and back to Peace.

Everything begins with the sentence "I want happiness". The practices that accompany it are *Contentment*, *Balance* and *Peaceful Meditation* (Thiền An); meditation is a supporting method for the journey towards peace within.

This white paper arranges those ideas into one coherent whole, adds commentary so they can be applied in families, schools, workplaces and communities, and sets out a roadmap by which the Peaceful Way could mature from a personal philosophy into a living tradition open to the whole world.

> **How to read this paper.** Passages marked *Source* are the words of Mr.33. Passages marked *Commentary* are interpretation and guidance for applying them. The two layers are marked throughout.

## 1. Why the Peaceful Way

People today have more than any generation before: more choices, more information, more comfort. But "having more" does not automatically become "being at peace". Most everyday unrest does not come from absolute lack. It comes from two inner movements:

- the feeling of *not enough*: I must have more, achieve more, hold on to more;
- the feeling of *I must be more*: I must be right, be recognised, never lose.

The Peaceful Way names these two movements Greed and Ego, and reverses the question of our age. Instead of "how do I get more?", it asks "how much is enough, and once I have enough, what do I live for?"

It does not ask anyone to leave ordinary life. It is designed for people who work, raise children, run businesses and study. It is peace *in the middle of* a changing life, not peace bought by escaping it.

## 2. Definitions and scope

**An** (peace) is a state of mind that is steady, clear and sufficient, whether or not circumstances go your way. An is not the absence of waves; it is having somewhere to stand when they come.

**Đạo An** is the way — a way of seeing, living and training — to return to that state and stay there.

The Peaceful Way **is**:

- a practical philosophy, verified by the practitioner's own experience;
- an ethical framework that begins with gratitude to one's roots;
- a set of simple methods anyone can do, with no equipment and no elaborate ritual.

The Peaceful Way **is not**:

- fortune-telling or prophecy — Kabala's principle is that *choice matters more than fate*;
- a substitute for medicine or psychotherapy;
- an exclusive creed — Buddhists, Christians, Muslims and people of no religion can all practise it;
- the veneration of any individual.

## 3. Diagnosis: the two roots of suffering

> **Source.** The Peaceful Way is founded on the recognition that all suffering in life originates from greed and the ego. Recognising and mastering greed and the ego is seen as essential to a happy and meaningful life. By reducing unnecessary desires and living humbly, we can find inner peace and harmony in our relationships with others.

### 3.1 Greed — "not enough"

> **Source.** Greed is excessive desire for material things, power, or recognition from others. It is not limited to money and property; it includes the craving for power, fame and attention. Greed means a person never feels satisfied with what they have and always wants more, which leads to unrest and suffering.

**Commentary.** Its mark is that joy always lives in the future and the present never gets its turn.

### 3.2 Ego — "I must be more"

> **Source.** The ego concerns the self, self-esteem, and how we perceive ourselves in relation to the world around us. An inflated ego can lead to selfishness, arrogance and a lack of empathy. When the ego rules, people place their own needs and wants above everything, which causes conflict and discord.

**Commentary.** Its marks are being easily hurt, comparing constantly, finding it hard to apologise and hard to listen.

### 3.3 Recognition table

| | Greed | Ego |
|---|---|---|
| Inner voice | "Not enough." | "I must be more / I must be right." |
| Common feelings | Craving, impatience, fear of loss | Wounded pride, envy, anger, shame |
| Behaviour | Hoarding, overwork, compensatory spending | Competing, defending, judging |
| Result | Never feeling complete | Isolation, broken relationships |
| Remedy | **Contentment** | **Humility and Hiếu (gratitude)** |

> **Commentary.** The Peaceful Way does not ask you to kill desire or erase yourself. Healthy desire (to eat, learn, build, love) and healthy self-respect are necessary. Trouble begins only when desire has no stopping point and when the self becomes the only measure. The task is to *notice* and *master*, not to suppress.

## 4. The core principle: Contentment

> **Source.** The core of the Peaceful Way is contentment: becoming aware of, and satisfied with, what one has, and from that creating peace and harmony in the soul and in life.

The rest of this section is commentary. Contentment is often mistaken for resignation. They differ at the root:

| | Contentment | Resignation |
|---|---|---|
| Comes from | Clarity and gratitude | Fear or giving up |
| Attitude to effort | Still does its best, without being bound to the result | Stops trying |
| Attitude to what one has | Values it and uses it well | Accepts it grudgingly |
| Outcome | Freedom, steadiness | Stagnation, regret |

Contentment consists of three trainable abilities:

1. **Seeing what is already here** — health, family, skills, time, today's meal.
2. **Setting the point of enough** — for each thing (money, work, social media, possessions), answering "how much is enough for me?"
3. **Stopping at enough** — and turning the surplus into giving.

## 5. Three purposes, one destination

> **Source.** The Path to Peace: *Know yourself* (awareness of your own needs, wants and limits); *True happiness* (finding joy and satisfaction in simple, practical things); *A meaningful life* (seeking and fulfilling your purpose through action and relationships with others).

> **Author's clarification (November 2026).** These are three purposes but one destination: reach self-understanding, and the other two are reached as well.

### 5.1 "I want happiness"

Anyone can say the sentence *I want happiness*. The Peaceful Way reads it like this:

- Dropping **I** is dropping the ego, the self.
- Dropping **Want** is dropping greed, and wishes for what does not suit you.
- Only **Happiness** remains, and it means happiness in the present, right now.

Happiness is therefore not a reward at the end of the road. It is what is left when the two words in front of it are put down.

### 5.2 The order of things

To drop "I" and "Want", one needs to understand the Circle of Hiếu Đạo, with An at its centre. Peaceful Meditation is a supporting method for understanding An through meditation. When we are at peace, we understand ourselves; from there we know what we "want", and so recognise true happiness, and we know what to let go of (the "I", the ego), and so find the meaning of life.

[[flow]]

| Knowing yourself, you know | What is let go | What appears |
|---|---|---|
| what you truly want | Want (greed, unsuitable wishes) | True happiness |
| what you need to let go | I (the ego) | A meaningful life |

> **Commentary.** Dropping "Want" does not mean having no wishes: a person who knows themselves sees more clearly what they truly want. Dropping "I" does not mean erasing yourself: what is let go is the inflated ego, selfish and lacking empathy.

## 6. The Circle of Hiếu Đạo

> **Source.** The Circle of Hiếu Đạo is the central concept of the Peaceful Way. It consists of Hiếu – Đạo – Đức – Phúc – Tài. Each aspect links harmoniously with the others and forms a continuous cycle between moral values and achievement in life. "An" is the root and the centre: everything good and of real value in life begins from a peaceful, still inner state. It is an endless cycle that always begins and ends at "An".

[[circle]]

An sits at the centre: both the starting point and the place of return. The five other words are five stages that peace passes through as it enters daily life.

### 6.1 Hiếu — putting one's parents at ease

- **Source:** respecting and loving one's parents, elders, and those who have done one good. Hiếu is not something forced by duty or by pressure from money and status; it is a serene inner state that lets one's parents feel at ease, and through that we find real peace ourselves.
- **Commentary:** Hiếu (often translated "filial piety") is also the first lesson that *I did not make myself*. In someone grateful to their roots, the Ego shrinks by itself. Extended, one's roots include parents, ancestors, teachers, benefactors, community, and the natural world that sustains us.
- **Practice:** call and listen to your parents; say specific thanks; care for them while you can; remember those who have died.
- **Avoid misreading:** Hiếu is not blind obedience, and it never obliges anyone to endure abuse. It is gratitude and responsibility, exercised with clear judgement.

### 6.2 Đạo — the Way

- **Source:** pursuing the path of ethics and ideals in life. Đạo is the way of living led by inner peace itself; a person who lives by it is not shaken by outside forces and stays steady and serene through hardship.
- **Commentary:** Đạo is a consciously chosen direction, replacing drift by habit or by the crowd.
- **Practice:** write down your three principles for living; before a major decision ask "is this on my path?"

### 6.3 Đức — Virtue

- **Source:** developing and maintaining noble moral qualities. Đức is formed from experience and deep self-awareness; it is not only outward good qualities but a balanced soul, kind and sincere with everyone.
- **Commentary:** Đạo is the direction; Đức is the footsteps. Virtue grows by repeating the right thing in small matters: honesty, keeping one's word, humility, kindness, patience.
- **Practice:** choose one quality to train each week; each evening review one thing done well and one thing to mend.

### 6.4 Phúc — Blessing

- **Source:** creating and experiencing blessings and happiness from ethical action. Phúc is not only material prosperity; above all it is serenity, fulfilment, harmony in every relationship, and deep meaning in life.
- **Commentary:** Phúc is not obtained by asking. It comes from Virtue: other people's trust, lasting relationships, sound sleep, a harmonious home. It is also what one leaves for those who come after.
- **Practice:** one kind act a day that nobody needs to know about.

### 6.5 Tài — Abundance through contentment

- **Source:** attaining wealth through contentment, not only in money but in every aspect of life. When Phúc is complete, Tài comes naturally; it is not only material but a richness of mind and feeling, and the ability to share with the community.
- **Commentary:** Tài comes last in the circle: wealth follows ethics and is defined by *enough*, not by *much*. Wealth earned honestly, used well and shared feeds Peace again; wealth with no stopping point pulls a person back into Greed.
- **Practice:** define your own "enough number"; set aside a fixed share to give; do not earn by means you are ashamed of.

### 6.6 Why a circle

Contented abundance returns us to Peace; deeper Peace makes Hiếu more genuine; and the circle turns again at a higher level. Conversely, wherever the circle breaks, suffering appears: wealth without virtue becomes disaster; virtue without gratitude becomes arrogance; duty without peace becomes a burden.

## 7. The three practices

### 7.1 Peaceful Meditation (Thiền An)

> **Source (Mr.33, Hanoi, 1 November 2024).** Peaceful Meditation joins meditation with the Peaceful Way. Its aim is not only stillness and balance of mind but a deeper view of happiness and the meaning of life.

It has three steps:

**Step 1 — Begin the spiritual journey.** Spirituality is the path by which a person can understand themselves, and from there find the meaning and purpose of life.

**Step 2 — Recognise the value of stillness.** Stillness is the state of the soul when there is no distraction and no activity of thought. The body always has a way of restoring its own balance; when we are too active, energy goes to those activities, and only when we stop does it turn to healing within. The most basic and important effect of stillness is to re-establish balance, which is to say, healing.

**Step 3 — Practise.**

1. **Any time:** at home or outside, in quiet or in noise, while doing something or doing nothing.
2. **Any posture:** walking, standing, lying down, sitting.
3. **Choose a way that suits you.**
   - *When doing nothing, or caught up in thoughts:* count silently and slowly 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, over and over, until the mind is empty; or silently repeat "A Di Đà Phật" (Amitabha) or "Om Mani Padme Hum"; or create a method of your own.
   - *When working:* give the task your whole attention, feeling each movement and each breath, without thinking about the result or the end. What matters is being fully present, here and now.
4. **Relax the mind:** when the mind starts to wander, bring it back to where it was focused.
5. **Listen:** after that there is nothing to do; just listen to your own body and mind.

Keep the practice going, continuously.

> **Commentary.** The source lists six values of stillness (peace, focus, awareness, capacity to cope, creativity, growth of the soul) and describes healing as the author understands it; this edition has not checked those statements against scientific research. A suggested dose for beginners: 5 minutes each morning, plus three "one-minute pauses" during the day.

### 7.2 Contentment (Biết Đủ)

> **Source.** Aim for gratitude and satisfaction with what you already have.

The exercises below are commentary:

- **Three enoughs each evening:** write three things you had enough of today.
- **The question before buying or taking on more:** "Do I need this, or want it? Once I have it, will I be more at peace?"
- **A no-more day:** one day a week with no shopping and no aimless scrolling.
- **Surplus into giving:** whatever exceeds your point of enough is shared.

### 7.3 Balance (Cân Bằng)

> **Source.** Maintain balance in life, from work to personal life.

> **Commentary.** Each week, score five areas from 1 to 5: *body* (sleep, food, movement), *mind* (stillness), *family*, *work*, *contribution*. The lowest score is where next week's time should go. Balance is not equal shares; it is not neglecting any area for too long.

## 8. Applications

### 8.1 The individual

Start from the sentence "I want happiness". Review regularly: what do I truly want, and what do I need to let go? Under stress, use the quick question: "Is this I or Want speaking?"

### 8.2 The family

Hiếu is the starting point: one shared meal a week with no phones; a habit of specific thanks; children taught *enough* before they are taught *more*. Most conflict at home is two Egos meeting. Whoever returns to Peace first listens first.

### 8.3 Education

Teach children three abilities: to name their feelings and wants; to thank those who helped them; to ask "how much is enough?" A minute of silence at the start of class is the simplest form of Peaceful Meditation.

### 8.4 Work and business

The Circle gives a clear order of priority: ethics first, profit second, and profit has a point of enough. For a company: gratitude to customers and staff (Hiếu), a clear mission (Đạo), trustworthiness (Đức), real value for society (Phúc), healthy growth (Tài).

### 8.5 Leadership

A leader at peace does not decide out of fear or pride. Practices: a one-minute pause before major decisions; admitting mistakes openly; measuring success by how much others grow.

### 8.6 Digital life

Social media amplifies both Greed (more to see, more to have) and Ego (comparison, the hunt for approval). Apply Contentment to attention: limit time, turn off unnecessary notifications, and ask "what am I opening this for?" before opening it.

### 8.7 Community and environment

Gratitude to nature, the shared root of everyone, leads to consumption with a point of enough. A community of people who know enough competes less and shares more.

## 9. In dialogue with other traditions

The Peaceful Way does not claim to be wholly new. It inherits the spirit of several traditions and rearranges it for people living ordinary lives today.

| Tradition | Where they meet | What is distinctive in the Peaceful Way |
|---|---|---|
| Buddhism (non-self, compassion, awakening) | Suffering is caused by craving and clinging to self; meditation is a method | No aim of leaving the world; the goal is peace inside ordinary life |
| Taoism (non-forcing, following nature) | Knowing what is enough; simplicity | Emphasises responsibility to family and society through Hiếu |
| Confucianism (benevolence, filial piety, rites) | Filial piety as the root of virtue; cultivate the self first | Hiếu grounded in gratitude, not hierarchy; adds an inner dimension (An) |
| Stoicism | Distinguishing what is in one's control; temperance | Family relationships are the starting point |

> **Commentary.** The Stoicism row is added by this edition to help international readers. A note on the name: Kabala's Đạo An is a modern concept and is unrelated to the Chinese Buddhist master Dao'an (312–385).

## 10. From a philosophy to a living tradition

A system of thought becomes a tradition — and, further, a religion — not by declaration, but because real people practise it over a long time and find their lives improved. The scholar of religion Ninian Smart described religions through seven dimensions. Using that frame as a self-audit:

| Dimension | What the Peaceful Way has now | What must be built |
|---|---|---|
| Doctrinal | Greed and Ego; "I want happiness"; three purposes, one destination; Contentment; the Circle | A stable, versioned root text; answers to the great questions (death, loss, injustice) |
| Ethical | Hiếu – Đạo – Đức – Phúc – Tài | A concrete code of conduct; guidance for hard cases |
| Practical and ritual | Peaceful Meditation, Contentment, Balance | A daily, weekly and yearly rhythm; rites for life's milestones (birth, coming of age, marriage, death) |
| Experiential | The peace found in practice | Honest accounts from practitioners; guidance for difficulties |
| Narrative | The founding story | A body of stories, parables and exemplary lives |
| Social and institutional | The Kabala ecosystem | Local practice groups; trained guides; transparent governance |
| Material and symbolic | The circle with An at the centre | Quiet spaces; publications; shared days of observance |

### Proposed roadmap

1. **Phase 1 — An open philosophy.** Complete the bilingual root text; publish it openly so anyone can read, translate and criticise it.
2. **Phase 2 — Communities of practice.** "Circles of An" of 5–12 people meeting weekly: ten minutes of Peaceful Meditation, sharing three enoughs, one act of Hiếu for the week.
3. **Phase 3 — Organisation.** Training guides; bylaws and open finances; a council that looks after the content.
4. **Phase 4 — Recognition.** Registration under each country's law once the community is stable.

> **Legal note.** Recognition of a religious organisation is governed by each country's law and usually requires years of stable activity, with clear teachings, purpose, a charter and an organisational structure. In Vietnam the governing statute is the 2016 Law on Belief and Religion. This section is orientation only, not legal advice; consult a lawyer before acting.

## 11. Nine safeguarding commitments

History shows that new spiritual movements easily slide into personality cults or profiteering. To deserve trust, this edition proposes nine commitments binding on any activity carried out in the name of the Peaceful Way:

1. **Voluntary.** No coercion, no pressure; anyone may come and go freely.
2. **No monopoly on truth.** Practitioners keep their own religion and beliefs.
3. **No personality cult.** The initiator and guides are companions, not objects of worship.
4. **Financial transparency.** The core teachings are always free; all contributions are voluntary and published.
5. **No selling of fear.** No threats about fate or disaster; no promises of miracles.
6. **No substitute for medicine.** People with physical or mental health problems need professional care.
7. **Protection of the vulnerable.** No tolerance of abuse in any form, including in the name of Hiếu.
8. **Respect for the law** of wherever one lives.
9. **Open to criticism.** The teachings are reviewed periodically; what is wrong gets corrected.

## 12. Limits and open questions

- The Peaceful Way is at an early stage; the source text (Mr.33, Việt Trì, 10 April 2025) describes itself as a draft still being completed.
- The source says the Peaceful Way not only helps individuals grow morally and spiritually but also aims at a peaceful, harmonious society; that social side has not yet been worked out in detail.
- No independent research has yet measured the effect of these practices. A sensible first step is to record practitioners' experience systematically.
- "All suffering comes from Greed and Ego" is a statement about *psychological* suffering. Pain from illness, poverty, violence and injustice must be met with practical action; the Peaceful Way helps us not to add inner suffering on top of it.
- Rites, stories and organisational structure still need to be built together by the community.

## Appendix B — One-day practice card

- **Morning:** 5 minutes of Peaceful Meditation before picking up your phone. One intention for the day.
- **During the day:** whatever you do, do it wholly. Under stress: count 1 to 6, ask "I or Want?"
- **Afternoon:** one act of Hiếu — a call, a thank-you.
- **Evening:** write three enoughs. One thing done well, one thing to mend.
